Summer of Science — Analog Electronics

8-week mentoring plan · Diodes → BJT → MOS → Op-amps → LTspice project

Mentor: Vedika  ·  Duration: 8 weeks (~6–8 hrs/week mentee effort)  ·  Goal: Build intuition for diodes, BJTs, MOSFETs and op-amps; finish with a non-trivial LTspice project that exercises both discrete and op-amp design.

Week 1

Linear network refresher + LTspice fluency

Concepts: KVL/KCL, Thevenin/Norton, superposition, RC transient & frequency response, phasors.
Why first: Everything later reduces to these. If shaky, op-amps will feel like magic.

LTspice lab

Week 8

Project + presentation

Final-project options

Pick by interest in Week 4; all scoped for ~4 weeks of part-time work.

  1. Audio preamp + Baxandall tone control. BJT CE input → op-amp Baxandall bass/treble → op-amp output buffer. Exercises biasing, gain staging, active filters, real op-amp limits. Reference: Franco Ch. 3 (single-amp filters).
  2. Function generator (sine / square / triangle). Op-amp relaxation oscillator → integrator (triangle) → diode-network sine shaper. Exercises Schmitt, integrator, diode shaping.
  3. Two-stage discrete amplifier with measured specs. CE → emitter follower. Target: Av ≥ 50, Zin ≥ 10 kΩ, Zout ≤ 100 Ω, BW ≥ 50 kHz, THD < 1% at 1 Vpp. Most rigorous.
  4. Thermistor / strain-gauge front-end. Wheatstone bridge → instrumentation amp → Sallen-Key anti-alias filter. Exercises CMRR, offset/drift, filtering. Closest to "real engineering."
My recommendation: #1 or #2 — fun, motivating, and the mentee genuinely hears the result (you can play a .wav into LTspice with a PWL file and look at the output).

LTspice things to teach explicitly

These trip everyone up — cover them deliberately rather than letting the mentee fight each one alone.

  • .tran 0 10m 0 1u — why max-step matters (default can alias HF content).
  • .ac dec <pts> <fstart> <fstop> — log sweep; cursors for −3 dB.
  • .dc V1 <start> <stop> <step> with nested .step param for family of curves.
  • .op for quick Q-point check.
  • .measure directives — e.g. .meas AC gain MAX V(out) for automated gain extraction.
  • Importing manufacturer models: .include / .lib, and adding symbols for parts not in the library (right-click → Pick New Symbol).
  • UniversalOpAmp2 parameters: Avol, GBW, SR, Vos — change to study non-idealities without changing the schematic.
  • Probing current through a component (Alt-click) vs voltage.
  • Plotting power: hold Alt while clicking a component to plot V·I.
  • Saving plot settings as .plt so reopening a sim gives the same view.

Pacing tips for you as mentor

  • Weekly 45-min call: mentee walks you through their LTspice file; you ask "what if I change X?" Don't lecture — debug.
  • Make them break things on purpose: remove the bypass cap, halve β, push input amplitude. Broken behaviour is where intuition lives.
  • Skip without guilt: deep semiconductor physics, h-parameters, manual high-frequency analysis past the dominant pole. Sim handles those.
  • Push for hand calc before every simulation. If sim disagrees with calc, that's the most valuable moment of the week — don't let them just trust the sim.
  • Backup resources: Razavi's Fundamentals of Microelectronics lectures on YouTube for intuition; Art of Electronics (Horowitz & Hill) for the "why does anyone do it this way" answers.
